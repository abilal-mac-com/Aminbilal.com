Markets and Money 26

Everything is liquid at the right price.

Trading-floor adage · origin untraced

Kin to the older dealers’ saying that everything is for sale at the right price; no coiner found as of 25 July 2026.

One of the funny things about the stock market is that every time one person buys, another sells, and both think they are astute.

William Feather

The Cleveland publisher-aphorist; primary locus unpinned as of 25 July 2026.

The market can stay irrational longer than you can stay solvent.

In print by 1986 and closely associated with Shilling, its likely coiner; routinely lent to Keynes, an ascription for which the evidence is very weak.

The famous “four most dangerous words” is the popular compression; this is the documented sentence. See the essay This Time Is Different.

All through time, people have basically acted and reacted the same way in the market as a result of greed, fear, ignorance, and hope. That is why the numerical formations and patterns recur on a constant basis. Over and over, with slight variations. Because markets are driven by humans and human nature never changes.

Jesse Livermore · How to Trade in Stocks, 1940

From the one book Livermore wrote himself, as anthologised (the 1940 text varies slightly); not to be confused with Reminiscences, which is Lefèvre’s.
